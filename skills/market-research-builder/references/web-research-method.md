# Web research method

1. Start with the approved research question and sanitized terms.
2. Prefer current official government and primary commercial sources.
3. Record exact URLs, titles, publication or effective dates, retrieval time, relevant excerpt as a paraphrased fact, and limits.
4. Confirm that a result applies to the correct product, service, version, geography, unit, quantity, period, and customer class.
5. Cross-check consequential claims and current prices.
6. Distinguish list, ceiling, discounted, quoted, historical, and paid prices.
7. Preserve zero-result and thin-result searches as evidence of search coverage, not proof that no source exists.
8. Never upload a user document or paste sensitive content into a search engine or public site.

A complete report normally needs evidence outside federal award history, such as commercial catalogs or quotes, supplier information, published market conditions, industry standards, and agency-specific material. If those sources are unavailable, label the result a federal-data desk-research draft.
